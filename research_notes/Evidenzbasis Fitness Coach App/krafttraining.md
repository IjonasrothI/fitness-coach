# Resistance Training for Trained Adults at Home with Minimal Equipment (≤2 kg DBs, 5 kg KB, loop bands, bodyweight)

Notes on method and verification
- Verified this session (primary abstract or bibliographic record seen): ACSM 2026 position stand (abstract text extracted from the open-access PDF), Robinson 2024, Pelland (preprint and Sports Med record), Singer 2024, Lopez 2021, Lopes 2019, Wolf 2023, Halperin 2022, Kassiano 2023, Pedrosa 2022, Pedrosa/Pereira/Kassiano 2026 review, Refalo 2023 (PMID only), Refalo 2024 (title only).

- Evidence-strength labels used: **MA** = meta-analysis or meta-regression; **SR** = systematic review without pooling; **OoR** = overview of reviews or position stand; **RCT** = randomized trial; **Cons** = consensus or guideline; **EO** = expert opinion or inference.

## 1. Weekly volume dose-response per muscle group

### Takeaway
Hypertrophy keeps rising with weekly sets but with diminishing returns. Strength plateaus at much lower volumes. A practical target for trained people is about 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week, counting indirect sets as 0.5. The ACSM 2026 stand supports ≥10 sets/week for hypertrophy (OoR). More than about 20 sets brings uncertain extra benefit and costs a lot of time.

### Cited Findings
- **ACSM 2026 (OoR, 137 SRs, >30,000 participants, healthy adults ≥18 y, RCTs ≥6 wk):** "Muscle hypertrophy was enhanced by higher volumes (≥10 sets/wk) and eccentric overload." Strength was enhanced by "2–3 sets" (per exercise or session, as stated in the abstract), ≥80% 1RM, full ROM, placement at the beginning of the session, and ≥2 sessions/wk. Citation: Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Fiatarone Singh MA, Lowisz CV, Rawson ES, Schoenfeld BJ, Smith-Ryan AE, Steen JP, Thomas GA, Triplett NT, Washington TA, Werner TJ, Phillips SM. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2026;58(4):851–872. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND). Data sources ran to October 2024. — [Open-access PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf); [Ovid record](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/acsm-msse/fulltext/10.1249/mss.0000000000003897~american-college-of-sports-medicine-position-stand)
- **Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC et al.:** "The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gain" (MA, 67 studies). SportRxiv preprint 2024; published in *Sports Medicine* 2026;56(2):481–505 (I could not confirm the DOI). It introduced "fractional" set counting: direct sets count 1 and indirect sets count 0.5. This method predicted outcomes best and was used for the primary models. Hypertrophy and volume showed a positive relationship with diminishing returns. Strength showed much more pronounced diminishing returns. — [SportRxiv preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460); [Sports Med record (sponet)](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139?lng=en)
- A secondary summary of the published Pelland paper says the authors found a low minimum effective dose (about 4 fractional sets per week) and no clear plateau in the primary hypertrophy model. This is second-hand and needs verification. — [search summary pointing to sponet / blog](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139)
- **Per-session volume (Remmert et al. 2025, SportRxiv preprint, same group):** Diminishing returns per session. The "point of undetectable outcome superiority" was about 2 direct sets per session for strength and about 11 fractional sets per session for hypertrophy. A search summary also attributes a figure of about 31 fractional sets per week, with no detectable superiority beyond it, to this preprint line; that attribution is ambiguous and needs verification. Not peer reviewed. — [SportRxiv preprint 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)
- **Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW (2017).** "Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass." *J Sports Sci* 35(11):1073–1082. DOI 10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197 (MA, 15 studies). A graded response was found: <5, 5–9 and ≥10 sets/wk per muscle. Each extra set added about 0.37% gain, and ≥10 sets/wk was best. Most participants were untrained or recreationally trained. — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Brad-Schoenfeld/publication/305455324_Dose-response_relationship_between_weekly_resistance_training_volume_and_increases_in_muscle_mass_A_systematic_review_and_meta-analysis/links/59dc0269458515e9ab4527d6/Dose-response-relationship-between-weekly-resistance-training-volume-and-increases-in-muscle-mass-A-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis.pdf) (effect sizes **[PK]**)
- **Ralston GW et al. (2017).** "The Effect of Weekly Set Volume on Strength Gain: A Meta-Analysis." *Sports Med* (MA). Strength gains were similar with low and moderate-to-high weekly volume. — [PubMed 28755103](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28755103/) (details **[PK]**)
- **Baz-Valle E, Balsalobre-Fernández C, Alix-Fages C, Santos-Concejero J (2022).** "A Systematic Review of The Effects of Different Resistance Training Volumes on Muscle Hypertrophy." *J Hum Kinet* 81:199–210. DOI 10.2478/hukin-2022-0017 (SR, trained participants only). About 12–20 weekly sets per muscle appears to be the range for hypertrophy in trained people. Evidence that >20 sets is better was inconsistent. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2022-0017) **[PK]**

### Inferences
- **App rule (MA + OoR):** Default to 10–16 fractional sets per muscle per week for trained users. Allow progression up to about 20. Offer a minimum dose of about 4–6 hard sets per muscle per week for busy or maintenance weeks.
- **App rule (MA, preprint):** Cap direct hard sets for one muscle at about 6–10 per session (from the per-session PUOS of about 11 fractional sets). Spread higher volumes over more days.
- **Set counting:** Count compound-exercise sets as 0.5 for synergists, for example a push-up counts 1 for chest and 0.5 for triceps (Pelland's "fractional" method).
- With very light external loads, a "set" only counts if it is close to failure (see section 2 and 3). Volume counting should be gated by effort.

### Gaps
- I did not read the published Pelland 2026 Sports Med full text. Exact per-set marginal gains and posterior probabilities are not verified.
- Volume dose-response data come almost entirely from gym (free-weight or machine) studies at moderate to high loads. None used band or bodyweight-only training in advanced trainees.

## 2. Frequency, proximity to failure (RIR), RIR self-rating accuracy, failure with light loads

### Takeaway
When volume is equated, frequency matters little for hypertrophy but somewhat for strength. Use it as a tool to distribute volume (≥2×/week per muscle). Hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure, while strength is fairly insensitive to it. A target of 0–3 RIR is appropriate. Momentary failure adds little on top of 1–2 RIR with moderate or heavy loads, but it appears to matter more with light loads. People typically misjudge RIR by about 1 rep, and they are more accurate near failure.

### Cited Findings
- **Pelland et al. (MA):** Only strength showed consistently identifiable frequency effects. For hypertrophy, frequency added little once volume was accounted for. — [SportRxiv preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)
- **ACSM 2026 (OoR):** ≥2 sessions/wk enhanced strength. "Training to momentary muscle fatigue, equipment type, exercise complexity, set structure, time under tension, blood flow restriction, and periodization did not consistently impact training outcomes." — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf)
- **Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Krieger J (2019).** "How many times per week should a muscle be trained to maximize muscle hypertrophy?" *J Sports Sci* 37(11):1286–1295. DOI 10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906 (MA). When volume was equated, frequency had no significant effect on hypertrophy. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906) **[PK]**
- **WHO 2020:** "Muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity that involve all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week" for adults. Bull FC et al. *Br J Sports Med* 2020;54:1451–1462. DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955 (Cons). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955) **[PK]**
- **Robinson ZP, Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Refalo MC, Jukic I, Steele J, Zourdos MC (2024).** "Exploring the Dose–Response Relationship Between Estimated Resistance Training Proximity to Failure, Strength Gain, and Muscle Hypertrophy: A Series of Meta-Regressions." *Sports Med* 54(9):2209–2231. DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2 (MA, about 55 studies). Strength gains were similar across a wide range of RIR. Hypertrophy improved as sets ended closer to failure. Model fit was modest, and RIR in the primary studies was estimated, so the exact relationship is unclear. — [Abertay record](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/); [FAU press release](https://www.fau.edu/newsdesk/articles/muscle-growth-strength-study)
- **Refalo MC, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Hamilton DL, Fyfe JJ (2023).** "Influence of Resistance Training Proximity-to-Failure on Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis." *Sports Med* 53(3):649–665. DOI 10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y (MA). Failure and non-failure training gave similar hypertrophy overall. The relationship with proximity may be non-linear. — [PubMed 36334240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/) (DOI **[PK]**)
- **Refalo MC et al. (2024).** "Similar muscle hypertrophy following eight weeks of resistance training to momentary muscular failure or with repetitions-in-reserve in resistance-trained individuals." *J Sports Sci* 42(1):85–101 (RCT, within-participant, trained men). Training to 0 RIR and to 1–2 RIR gave similar quadriceps hypertrophy. — [search record / SportRxiv](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/484/1032/967) (volume and pages **[PK]**)
- **Halperin I, Malleron T, Har-Nir I, Androulakis-Korakakis P, Wolf M, Fisher J, Steele J (2022).** "Accuracy in Predicting Repetitions to Task Failure in Resistance Exercise: A Scoping Review and Exploratory Meta-analysis." *Sports Med* 52(2):377–390. DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01559-x. PMID 34542869 (MA). On average people underpredicted reps to failure by about 0.95 reps. Accuracy improved closer to failure. Training status did not appear to matter, and set number had a trivial effect. — [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/accuracy-in-predicting-repetitions-to-task-failure-in-resistance-/) (DOI **[PK]**)
- **Lasevicius T, Schoenfeld BJ, Silva-Batista C, et al. (2022).** "Muscle Failure Promotes Greater Muscle Hypertrophy in Low-Load but Not in High-Load Resistance Training." *J Strength Cond Res* 36(2):346–351. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003454 (RCT, untrained). At 30% 1RM, failure produced more hypertrophy than volume-matched non-failure training. At 80% 1RM it made no difference. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000003454) **[PK]**
- **RIR-based RPE scale:** Zourdos MC et al. (2016). *J Strength Cond Res* 30(1):267–275. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001049 (validation study). RIR ratings were more accurate in experienced lifters and closer to failure. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000001049) **[PK]**

### Inferences
- **Frequency rule (MA + Cons):** Train each muscle ≥2×/week. Split weekly volume so no session exceeds about 6–10 hard sets per muscle. Three or more sessions per muscle per week is fine and convenient at home.
- **Effort rule (MA + RCT):** Default target is 1–3 RIR. Allow 0 RIR (failure) on the last set of an exercise, especially on isolation and band exercises where failure is safe. Avoid failure on risky movements such as pistols with balance demands or Nordics late in a set.
- **Light-load rule (RCT, untrained, PK):** When external load is very light (about ≤30% 1RM, or sets above about 25–30 reps), require the set to go to failure or 0–1 RIR. Otherwise the set should not count as a full hypertrophy set.
- **RIR calibration (MA):** RIR guesses miss by about 1 rep on average and are worse far from failure. Every 1–2 weeks the app should prompt one "calibration set" to failure per exercise and compare predicted with actual reps. Shift future RIR targets by the observed bias.

### Gaps
- Robinson 2024 does not show a precise RIR-to-hypertrophy curve for low-load or band training in trained people.
- I found no data on RIR accuracy specifically with elastic bands or high-rep bodyweight sets, where accuracy is plausibly worse. Halperin 2022 found accuracy worse with higher-rep sets **[PK, not re-verified]**.

## 3. Low-load training: effectiveness, minimum load thresholds, discomfort limits

### Takeaway
When sets go to or near failure, hypertrophy is largely load-independent, from about 30% 1RM upwards (MA, OoR). Strength (1RM) gains favour heavier loads because of specificity. Below about 20–30% 1RM, hypertrophy evidence weakens, and very high-rep sets become limited by discomfort and cardiorespiratory or metabolic factors. For trained adults with 2–5 kg, most lower-body and many upper-body exercises fall far below 30% 1RM. Bodyweight, unilateral work, leverage changes and bands must restore an adequate relative load. In practice, aim for failure within about 5–30 reps.

### Cited Findings
- **Lopez P, Radaelli R, Taaffe DR, Newton RU, Galvão DA, Trajano GS, Teodoro JL, Kraemer WJ, Häkkinen K, Pinto RS (2021).** "Resistance Training Load Effects on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gain: Systematic Review and Network Meta-analysis." *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 53(6):1206–1216. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002585 (network MA, 28 studies, 747 healthy adults, all to volitional failure). Loads were classed as low (>15RM), moderate (9–15RM) and high (≤8RM). Hypertrophy appeared load-independent. Strength was greater with high loads (SMD about 0.60–0.63) and moderate loads (about 0.34–0.35) than with low loads. — [PubMed 33433148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33433148/); [JYX repository](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_75929) (DOI **[PK]**)
- **ACSM 2026 (OoR):** Strength was enhanced by ≥80% 1RM loads. A secondary summary reports that the stand found hypertrophy similar across about 30–100% 1RM "provided effort was sufficient". That range is not in the abstract and needs verification in the full text. — [PDF abstract](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf); [blog summary](https://www.moveyourbonespt.com/blog/2026-acsm-resistance-training-guidelines)
- **Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Ogborn D, Krieger JW (2017).** "Strength and Hypertrophy Adaptations Between Low- vs. High-Load Resistance Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis." *J Strength Cond Res* 31(12):3508–3523. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000002200 (MA). Hypertrophy was similar with low loads (≤60% 1RM) and high loads when training went to failure. 1RM gains favoured high loads. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002200) **[PK]**
- **Schoenfeld BJ, Peterson MD, Ogborn D, Contreras B, Sonmez GT (2015).** *J Strength Cond Res* 29(10):2954–2963. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000000958 (RCT, **trained men**). 25–35RM sets to failure produced hypertrophy similar to 8–12RM. 1RM gains were greater with heavy loads. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000958) **[PK]**
- **Lasevicius T, Ugrinowitsch C, Schoenfeld BJ, et al. (2018).** "Effects of different intensities of resistance training with equated volume load on muscle strength and hypertrophy." *Eur J Sport Sci* 18(6):772–780. DOI 10.1080/17461391.2018.1450898 (RCT, untrained). 20% 1RM produced clearly less hypertrophy than 40%, 60% and 80% 1RM, which were similar. This suggests a threshold somewhere between 20% and 40% 1RM. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2018.1450898) **[PK]**
- **Plotkin D, Coleman M, Van Every D, et al., Schoenfeld BJ (2022).** "Progressive overload without progressing load? The effects of load or repetition progression on muscular adaptations." *PeerJ* 10:e14142. DOI 10.7717/peerj.14142 (RCT, **trained**). Progressing reps instead of load gave similar hypertrophy. 1RM squat gains were slightly favoured by load progression. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.14142) **[PK]**

### Inferences
- **Minimum relative-load rule (RCT, EO):** An exercise variant is "valid for hypertrophy" if the user reaches 0–2 RIR within about 30 reps (roughly ≥30–40% 1RM). If the user can exceed about 30 reps, the app must move them to a harder variant (unilateral, leverage, band, deficit, slower eccentric, pause) and not just add reps.
- **Target rep window (EO, derived from MA):** For home training use about 6–30 reps to near failure. About 8–20 is the comfort sweet spot. Below about 6 reps on bodyweight progressions such as one-arm push-ups or pistols, the main limit is skill and stability. Above about 30 reps, discomfort and pacing dominate.
- **Strength expectation (MA, OoR):** Tell users that maximal-strength gains, as tested in 1RM, will be smaller than in heavy-load gym training. Strength measured on the trained exercise (rep max on the variant) will still improve. Track performance on the trained variant and not hypothetical 1RM.

### Gaps
- I found no robust meta-analytic threshold for minimum %1RM in trained people. The 20–30% figure comes mainly from single RCTs in untrained participants.
- I found no well-cited sources quantifying the discomfort or RPE burden of high-rep sets in this session. Older work (e.g. Fisher/Steele) reports higher discomfort with low loads to failure **[PK, not verified]**.

## 4. Elastic bands vs free weights or machines; quantifying band load and progression

### Takeaway
Elastic resistance produces strength gains similar to conventional weights in mixed, mostly untrained or older populations (MA). The ACSM 2026 stand found that equipment type did not consistently affect outcomes (OoR). Direct hypertrophy comparisons in trained people are scarce. Band tension depends on elongation, so the app should track band colour or level, the anchor or stance (starting stretch), reps and RIR. It should not try to estimate kg.

### Cited Findings
- **Lopes JSS, Machado AF, Micheletti JK, de Almeida AC, Cavina AP, Pastre CM (2019).** "Effects of training with elastic resistance versus conventional resistance on muscular strength: A systematic review and meta-analysis." *SAGE Open Med* 7:2050312119831116. DOI 10.1177/2050312119831116 (MA, 8 studies). Training with elastic devices (tubes, Thera-Band) and with machines or dumbbells gave similar upper- and lower-limb strength gains. An erratum swapped the limb effect sizes. Corrected values are lower limb SMD −0.11 and upper limb SMD 0.09, and the conclusion is unchanged. Populations were heterogeneous and mostly not trained lifters. — [PMC6383082](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6383082); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/f76933a02f3a4ff08fd3d81de9169831)
- **ACSM 2026 (OoR):** "equipment type ... did not consistently impact training outcomes." A secondary summary says the stand reports gains across elastic-band, home-based and gym-based training. — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf); [blog](https://www.moveyourbonespt.com/blog/2026-acsm-resistance-training-guidelines)
- **Calatayud J, Borreani S, Colado JC, Martin F, Tella V, Andersen LL (2015).** "Bench press and push-up at comparable levels of muscle activity results in similar strength gains." *J Strength Cond Res* 29(1):246–253. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000000589 (RCT, trained-ish university students). Band-resisted push-ups matched to 6RM gave 1RM bench gains similar to bench press. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000589) **[PK]**
- **Kikuchi N, Nakazato K (2017).** "Low-load bench press and push-up induce similar muscle hypertrophy and strength gain." *J Exerc Sci Fit* 15(1):37–42. DOI 10.1016/j.jesf.2017.06.003 (RCT). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesf.2017.06.003) **[PK]**
- **Band load quantification:** Uchida MC et al. (2016). "Thera-band® elastic band tension: reference values for physical activity." *J Phys Ther Sci* 28(4):1266–1271. DOI 10.1589/jpts.28.1266 (measurement study). Tension rises roughly linearly with % elongation and differs by colour. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1589/jpts.28.1266) **[PK]**
- **Colado JC, Garcia-Masso X, Triplett NT, et al. (2012).** "Concurrent validation of the OMNI-Resistance Exercise Scale of perceived exertion with Thera-Band resistance bands." *J Strength Cond Res* 26(11):3018–3024. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e318245c0c9 (validation study). Perceived exertion scales are valid for prescribing band intensity. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e318245c0c9) **[PK]**
- A 2025 meta-analysis on elastic band training in older adults reports benefits for lower-limb strength and balance, but it was not a comparison against conventional training. — [PMC12630110](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12630110/)
- "Lu 2022" meta-analysis: **not found** by search (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **Band equivalence rule (MA + OoR, mostly untrained):** Treat band exercises as equivalent to free-weight or machine exercises for volume counting, provided RIR is 0–3 within the valid rep window.
- **Band load model (EO + measurement studies):** Log each band set as (band level, anchor position or stance width or starting stretch, doubled or single band, reps, RIR). Use a progression ladder in this order: more reps, then more pre-stretch (step further from the anchor), then a stronger band, then combined bands, then unilateral. Do not convert to kg, because manufacturer kg values depend on elongation and differ between brands.
- **Strength curve:** Bands load the shortened position most and the lengthened position least. This works against the lengthened-position stimulus (section 5). Combine bands with bodyweight or dumbbells, or add pre-stretch so tension in the lengthened position is meaningful.
- Loop bands wear and lose tension over time. Prompt users to recalibrate (rep-max test) when they replace a band.

### Gaps
- I found no meta-analysis comparing band and free-weight **hypertrophy** in **trained** adults.
- I could not identify the "Lu 2022" meta-analysis. The coordinator may have a title or DOI; otherwise drop it.

## 5. Progression without adding load

### Takeaway
Several load-free levers have reasonable evidence. Rep progression is about as good as load progression for hypertrophy (RCT, trained). Training at long muscle lengths or lengthened partials looks favourable (MA of few studies, plus RCTs, plus a 2026 review). Tempo has little effect within about 0.5–8 s per rep (MA). Drop sets and rest-pause give similar hypertrophy in less time (MA). Unilateral and leverage-based exercise progressions make light-load training viable. Eccentric overload is one of the few variables the ACSM 2026 stand flagged for hypertrophy.

### Cited Findings
- **Rep progression:** Plotkin et al. 2022 *PeerJ* (RCT, trained). Progressing reps gave similar hypertrophy to progressing load. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.14142) **[PK]**
- **Partial vs full ROM:** Wolf M, Androulakis-Korakakis P, Fisher J, Schoenfeld B, Steele J (2023). "Partial Vs Full Range of Motion Resistance Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." *Int J Strength Cond* 3(1). DOI 10.47206/ijsc.v3i1.182 (Bayesian MA). Overall SMD was 0.12 (95% CI −0.02 to 0.26), a trivial effect favouring full ROM. A small subset of studies (about 3) comparing **lengthened partials** with full ROM tended to favour lengthened partials for hypertrophy. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/99e86b317bec40f5abc2f0008358737e) (DOI **[PK]**)
- **Pedrosa GF, Lima FV, Schoenfeld BJ, et al. (2022).** "Partial range of motion training elicits favorable improvements in muscular adaptations when carried out at long muscle lengths." *Eur J Sport Sci* 22(8):1250–1260. DOI 10.1080/17461391.2021.1927199 (RCT, untrained women, knee extension). Partials at long muscle lengths gave more quadriceps hypertrophy than partials at short lengths and matched or exceeded full ROM. — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Gustavo-Pedrosa/publication/351538891_Partial_range_of_motion_training_elicits_favorable_improvements_in_muscular_adaptations_when_carried_out_at_long_muscle_lengths/links/61f1e6bbc5e3103375c2e9f9/Partial-range-of-motion-training-elicits-favorable-improvements-in-muscular-adaptations-when-carried-out-at-long-muscle-lengths.pdf) (issue and pages **[PK]**; search returned conflicting volume and issue data)

Lengthened-partial calf raises gave more medial and lateral gastrocnemius growth than full or short-length ROM. — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Witalo-Kassiano-2/publication/365127382_Greater_gastrocnemius_muscle_hypertrophy_after_partial_range_of_motion_training_performed_at_long_muscle_lengths/links/64e9ce1a434d3f628c4e27f8/Greater-Gastrocnemius-Muscle-Hypertrophy-After-Partial-Range-of-Motion-Training-Performed-at-Long-Muscle-Lengths.pdf)

- **Wolf et al. 2025 (PeerJ, within-participant RCT):** Lengthened partials and full ROM gave similar strength-endurance adaptations. Results across studies are mixed. — [PeerJ 18904](https://peerj.com/articles/18904)
- **Tempo / time under tension:** Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn DI, Krieger JW (2015). "Effect of repetition duration during resistance training on muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Sports Med* 45(4):577–585. DOI 10.1007/s40279-015-0304-0 (MA). Very slow (>10 s) reps may be inferior. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-015-0304-0) **[PK]**. **ACSM 2026:** time under tension did not consistently affect outcomes (OoR). — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf)
- **Eccentric overload:** ACSM 2026 lists eccentric overload as enhancing hypertrophy (OoR). — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf)
- **Nordic hamstring exercise:** van Dyk N, Behan FP, Whiteley R (2019). "Including the Nordic hamstring exercise in injury prevention programmes halves the rate of hamstring injuries." *Br J Sports Med* 53(21):1362–1370. DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2018-100045 (MA, athletes). About 51% fewer hamstring injuries. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-100045) **[PK]**
- **Drop sets:** Sødal LK, Kristiansen E, Larsen S, van den Tillaar R (2023). "Effects of Drop Sets on Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis." *Sports Med Open* 9:66. DOI 10.1186/s40798-023-00620-5 (MA). Drop sets gave hypertrophy similar to traditional sets in less time. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00620-5) **[PK]**. **ACSM 2026:** "set structure" did not consistently affect outcomes (OoR). — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf)
- **Unilateral vs bilateral:** Speirs DE, Bennett MA, Finn CV, Turner AP (2016). "Unilateral vs. bilateral squat training for strength, sprints, and agility in academy rugby players." *J Strength Cond Res* 30(2):386–392. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001096 (RCT, trained athletes). Rear-foot-elevated split squat gave strength gains similar to back squat. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000001096) **[PK]**
- **Blood flow restriction (safety):** Patterson SD, Hughes L, Warmington S, et al. (2019). "Blood Flow Restriction Exercise: Considerations of Methodology, Application, and Safety." *Front Physiol* 10:533. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2019.00533 (Cons). BFR uses 20–40% 1RM with individualized limb occlusion pressure (40–80%). It requires screening for thrombosis or cardiovascular risk and proper cuffs. **ACSM 2026:** BFR did not consistently affect outcomes (OoR). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2019.00533) **[PK]**; [ACSM PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Progression hierarchy for the app (EO built on RCT/MA):**
  1. Add reps within the rep window (double progression), for example 8–15 or 12–25 reps.
  2. At the top of the window, increase difficulty with a harder variant (leverage, unilateral, deficit or ROM, band pre-stretch or stronger band) and reset to the bottom of the window.
  3. Use intensifiers on the last set when no harder variant exists: lengthened partials after failure, rest-pause or myo-reps, mechanical drop sets (harder variant to failure, then an easier one), 2–4 s eccentrics, 1–2 s pause in the stretched position.
- **Exercise progression ladders (EO):**
  - Push: incline push-up → push-up → deficit push-up (on DBs) → feet-elevated → band-resisted → archer → one-arm (negatives).
  - Legs: goblet squat (5 kg) → split squat → rear-foot-elevated (Bulgarian) split squat (+KB, +deficit, +1.5 reps) → skater squat or shrimp squat → pistol.
  - Hamstrings: hip-hinge with band → single-leg RDL (KB) → sliding leg curl (towel) → single-leg sliding curl → Nordic negatives.
  - Calves: single-leg deficit calf raise with stretch pause.
  - Pull: see section 8.
- **Tempo rule (MA):** Keep tempo between 1–4 s eccentric. Use a slower eccentric (3–4 s) and a pause in the stretch as progression tools, not ultra-slow reps (>8–10 s total).
- **Lengthened-bias rule (RCT + review):** Prefer variants with high torque in the stretched position, such as deficit push-ups, deep split squats, overhead triceps extension, incline curls and stretch-pause calf raises. Lengthened partials are a valid last-set intensifier.
- **BFR:** The app should **not** program BFR. At most it should add a safety note: proper cuffs, pressure individualization, contraindications (DVT history, hypertension, pregnancy). Improvised bands or straps as cuffs are not recommended **(Cons, PK)**.
- **Nordic note:** Strong eccentric stimulus, high soreness. Introduce at low volume (1–2 sets of 3–5 negatives) and progress slowly **(EO)**.

### Gaps
- Myo-reps and rest-pause: I found no dedicated meta-analysis this session. Evidence is from a few RCTs (e.g. Prestes et al. 2019, JSCR **[PK, not verified]**). The ACSM 2026 "set structure" statement covers it broadly.
- Lengthened-partials evidence is mostly from untrained or young women and from a handful of exercises (knee extension, calves, elbow flexors). Generalization to all muscles is uncertain.
- Mechanical drop sets and bodyweight progression ladders have **no** specific outcome trials. They are expert opinion.

## 6. Rest intervals, set structure, exercise order

### Takeaway
Rest of more than about 60 s, ideally about 90–120 s or longer for compound movements, gives a small hypertrophy advantage, likely via maintained volume load. Beyond about 90 s there are no clear differences (MA). Exercises prioritised for strength should come first (OoR). Set structure such as drop sets, supersets or rest-pause does not consistently change outcomes (OoR). Antagonist supersets can save time.

### Cited Findings
- **Singer A, Wolf M, Generoso L, Arias E, Delcastillo K, Echevarria E, Martinez A, Androulakis Korakakis P, Refalo MC, Swinton PA, Schoenfeld BJ (2024).** "Give it a rest: a systematic review with Bayesian meta-analysis on the effect of inter-set rest interval duration on muscle hypertrophy." *Front Sports Act Living* 6:1429789. DOI 10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789 (Bayesian MA, 9 studies, 19 measurements, healthy adults). There was a small hypertrophic benefit with rest >60 s, perhaps mediated by maintained volume load, and no appreciable difference beyond about 90 s. Proximity to failure did not modify the effect. Credible intervals were wide. Note: the paper is in Front Sports Act Living, not Sports Med Open. — [PubMed 39205815](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/); [Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/abstract)
- **Schoenfeld BJ, Pope ZK, Benik FM, et al. (2016).** "Longer Interset Rest Periods Enhance Muscle Strength and Hypertrophy in Resistance-Trained Men." *J Strength Cond Res* 30(7):1805–1812. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001272 (RCT, **trained**). 3 min beat 1 min for strength and some hypertrophy outcomes. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000001272) **[PK]**
- **ACSM 2026 (OoR):** Strength was enhanced when exercises were "at the beginning of training sessions". Set structure was not consistently influential. — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Rest rule (MA + RCT):** Use 60–90 s for isolation or band exercises and 90–180 s for hard compound or unilateral leg exercises. Do not penalize users for longer rest. Offer an "efficiency mode" with antagonist supersets or alternating limbs (rest one side while working the other) to keep sessions short.
- **Order rule (OoR):** Put the hardest skill or strength-progression exercise first, for example one-arm push-up negatives, pistols or Nordics. Put band isolation and finishers (myo-reps, lengthened partials) last.
- With light loads, sets are often limited by metabolic fatigue. Short rest further reduces reps and volume quality. This supports at least 60 s rest **(EO from Singer 2024 mechanism)**.

### Gaps
- Rest-interval data at very low loads or with bands specifically were not found.

## 7. Autoregulation and plateau management

### Takeaway
RIR/RPE-based autoregulation performs at least as well as fixed percentage-based programming (RCT, MA). With no meaningful load increments available, double progression with RIR targets is the core engine. Plateau detection must rely on reps at a given variant and RIR, plus variant upgrades. Velocity-based training is not applicable.

### Cited Findings
- **Helms ER, Byrnes RK, Cooke DM, et al. (2018).** "RPE vs. Percentage 1RM Loading in Periodized Programs Matched for Sets and Repetitions." *Front Physiol* 9:247. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2018.00247 (RCT, trained men). RPE-based loading gave similar or slightly better strength gains and similar hypertrophy. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2018.00247) **[PK]**
- **Hickmott LM, Chilibeck PD, Shaw KA, Butcher SJ (2022).** "The Effect of Load and Volume Autoregulation on Muscular Strength and Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." *Sports Med Open* 8:9. DOI 10.1186/s40798-021-00404-9 (MA). Autoregulated load produced strength gains similar or slightly better than fixed loading. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-021-00404-9) **[PK]**
- **ACSM 2026 (OoR):** Periodization did not consistently affect outcomes. Healthy adults "should perform progressive RT, with variable prescription". Secondary summaries say the stand emphasizes individualization and adherence over exact prescription variables. — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf); [physio-pedia summary (403 on fetch; search snippet)](https://members.physio-pedia.com/members-news/new-acsm-resistance-training-guidelines-what-physiotherapists-need-to-know/)
- **RIR accuracy:** RIR estimates miss by about 1 rep on average and improve near failure (Halperin 2022, MA). — [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/accuracy-in-predicting-repetitions-to-task-failure-in-resistance-/)
- **Rep progression is valid:** Plotkin 2022 (RCT, trained). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.14142) **[PK]**

### Inferences (app rules, EO unless stated)
- **Double progression:** Each exercise gets a rep window, for example 8–15. When all working sets reach the top of the window at the target RIR (e.g. ≤2), upgrade the variant or band and reset to the bottom.
- **Performance metric:** Track reps × variant level × RIR. Optionally estimate "reps to failure" as reps + RIR to create a comparable score per variant.
- **Plateau detection:** Flag a plateau when there is no increase in reps-to-failure on the same variant over 3 consecutive exposures, or about 2–3 weeks, while RIR is reported honestly. Response options in order: (1) check sleep, stress and recovery, and offer a deload of about 1 week at about 50% of sets; (2) swap the variant or exercise (different angle or lengthened bias); (3) add 1–2 sets per week for that muscle, up to the cap of about 20 fractional sets; (4) change the rep window.
- **Recovery check:** If performance drops in 2 consecutive sessions, or soreness is high, reduce volume by 20–30% for a week.
- **Deloads:** Expert practice is every 4–8 weeks, or reactively based on performance. I found no direct meta-analytic support in this session.

### Gaps
- I found no studies on plateau definitions or autoregulation algorithms specifically for bodyweight or band training.
- I did not search the deload evidence (e.g. Bell et al. 2023/2024 Delphi or surveys) this session.

## 8. Current position stands (ACSM 2026, WHO 2020, NSCA)

### Takeaway
The **ACSM 2026 position stand exists**: Currier et al., *MSSE* 58(4):851–872, open access, an overview of 137 systematic reviews. It replaces the 2009 progression-models stand. Its central message is that few prescription variables matter much. Strength responds to heavy loads (≥80% 1RM), full ROM, 2–3 sets, being placed early in the session and ≥2×/week. Hypertrophy responds to ≥10 sets/week and eccentric overload. Failure, equipment type, set structure, tempo, BFR and periodization did not consistently matter. WHO 2020 sets the health baseline: muscle-strengthening ≥2 days/week for all major muscle groups.

### Cited Findings
- **ACSM 2026:** Full citation and abstract numbers as in sections 1–7. Authors are Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Fiatarone Singh MA, Lowisz CV, Rawson ES, Schoenfeld BJ, Smith-Ryan AE, Steen JP, Thomas GA, Triplett NT, Washington TA, Werner TJ, Phillips SM. Literature searches ran to October 2024. The paper was submitted February 2025 and accepted September 2025. Abstract quote: "Few RT prescription (RTx) variables affected primary adaptations." Power was enhanced by 30–70% 1RM loads, low-to-moderate volume (≤24 reps × sets), Olympic lifting and a fast concentric phase. — [Open-access PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf); [Stuart Phillips on X](https://x.com/mackinprof/status/2032979481896288756)
- A secondary blog attributes a near-failure figure of about 2–3 RIR to the stand ("every set to absolute failure is not required"). The abstract only says failure did not consistently affect outcomes. — [blog](https://www.moveyourbonespt.com/blog/2026-acsm-resistance-training-guidelines)
- **WHO 2020 Guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour:** adults should do muscle-strengthening at moderate or greater intensity for all major muscle groups on ≥2 days/week. Bull FC et al. *Br J Sports Med* 2020;54:1451–1462 (Cons). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955) **[PK]**
- **NSCA:** I did not search for a 2020–2026 NSCA position stand on general resistance-training prescription in this session. NSCA's classic guidance (Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning) uses %1RM and rep-range zones **[PK]**.

### Inferences
- The ACSM 2026 "few variables matter" conclusion supports an app design that prioritizes adherence, enough weekly hard sets, effort near failure and progression over complex periodization.
- The strength benefit of ≥80% 1RM cannot be achieved with 2–5 kg for most muscles. It is only partially achievable with hard bodyweight progressions such as one-arm push-ups, pistols and Nordics, which can be about 5–10RM efforts. These are the "heavy" options in the app.

### Gaps
- I did not read the ACSM 2026 full-text evidence statements and certainty grades, because the PDF text extraction failed beyond page 1. The DOI is open access, so the report writer can access it.
- NSCA: no current general position stand was verified.

## 9. Hard-to-train muscle groups with light equipment and solutions

### Takeaway
The hardest areas are (1) **back and pulling** (lats, rhomboids, rear delts, biceps), because no bar or rings are available, and (2) **legs** for trained people (quads, glutes, hamstrings), because 5 kg is trivial for bilateral exercises. Solutions: unilateral and leverage progressions for legs, hamstring sliders and Nordics, and band-based pulling via a door anchor or a band looped around a fixed object, plus towel or door-frame rows. Evidence for specific home-pulling solutions is limited to EMG and expert opinion.

### Cited Findings
- **Legs:** Unilateral rear-foot-elevated split squat matched bilateral back squat for strength in trained athletes (RCT). — Speirs 2016, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000001096) **[PK]**
- Lengthened-position emphasis benefits quads and calves (RCTs). — [Pedrosa 2022](https://researchgate.net/profile/Gustavo-Pedrosa/publication/351538891_Partial_range_of_motion_training_elicits_favorable_improvements_in_muscular_adaptations_when_carried_out_at_long_muscle_lengths/links/61f1e6bbc5e3103375c2e9f9/Partial-range-of-motion-training-elicits-favorable-improvements-in-muscular-adaptations-when-carried-out-at-long-muscle-lengths.pdf); [Kassiano 2023](https://researchgate.net/profile/Witalo-Kassiano-2/publication/365127382_Greater_gastrocnemius_muscle_hypertrophy_after_partial_range_of_motion_training_performed_at_long_muscle_lengths/links/64e9ce1a434d3f628c4e27f8/Greater-Gastrocnemius-Muscle-Hypertrophy-After-Partial-Range-of-Motion-Training-Performed-at-Long-Muscle-Lengths.pdf)
- **Hamstrings:** Nordic hamstring exercise is effective for eccentric strength and injury prevention (MA). — van Dyk 2019, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-100045) **[PK]**
- **Push or chest:** Band-resisted push-ups match bench press for strength (RCT). — Calatayud 2015, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000589) **[PK]**
- **Equipment-agnostic outcomes:** ACSM 2026 (OoR). — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Prescription-for-Muscl.pdf)
- **Pulling with bands:** I found no RCTs or meta-analyses this session that compared band rows or pulldowns with cable or bar pulling for hypertrophy in trained people. See Gaps.

### Inferences (EO, building on the above)
- **Back and pulling toolkit:**
  - Band lat pulldown or straight-arm pulldown from a high door anchor (kneeling, single-arm for more tension).
  - Band seated or standing row from a low or mid anchor (single-arm, step further back for pre-stretch).
  - Band face-pulls and pull-aparts for rear delts.
  - Towel or door-frame rows (bodyweight, feet closer for harder).
  - Table inverted rows only if a sturdy table is available; add a safety warning.
  - Prone Y-T-W raises with 2 kg for scapular muscles.
  - Biceps: band curls plus 2 kg incline or lengthened-position curls taken to failure.
  - A door-mounted pull-up bar is the single most valuable cheap add-on. The app may suggest it as optional.
- **Legs toolkit:** Bulgarian split squat with KB, deficit reverse lunge, skater or shrimp squat, pistol progression, sissy squat or spanish squat with a band for quads, single-leg hip thrust or glute bridge (band around knees or across hips), single-leg RDL with KB, sliding leg curls, Nordic negatives, single-leg deficit calf raises. Use tempo, 1.5 reps, pause and lengthened partials to stay within the ≤30-rep window.
- **Shoulders:** Band lateral raises (cross-body anchor under foot), 2 kg lateral raises to failure (high reps OK for side delts), pike push-up → elevated pike → handstand push-up negatives.
- **Triceps:** Overhead band extension (long muscle length), close-grip or diamond push-ups.
- **Core:** Bodyweight progressions are sufficient (planks, hollow holds, ab-wheel substitutes with towel slides).
- **Rule:** For each muscle, the app needs at least one variant that brings the user to failure within about 30 reps at their current level. If none exists, flag the muscle as "under-loaded" and recommend an equipment add-on (heavier band, door pull-up bar, heavier KB).

### Gaps
- I found no direct trials on band door-anchor pulling vs bar or cable pulling for hypertrophy in trained adults.
- I found no trials on the 5 kg kettlebell or 2 kg dumbbell configuration specifically. All recommendations here are extrapolated from load-independence (failure-dependent) evidence.
- Safety data on door anchors (failure or rebound injuries) were not searched.
